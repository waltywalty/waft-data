goal round · research round 16 · August 2026

Eleven batteries, one survivor, three exposed myths

The standing goal: five upgrades or three intertwinable strategies, at the house evidence bar. Round 16 ran two sweeps — four batteries derived from our own meta-law and the regime literature, then seven from a deep dive into what demonstrably successful traders publish. Every battery pre-registered in reference/goal_ledger.md; ~58 cells; new data acquired: VIX and VIX3M (official CBOE mirrors), USDJPY intraday (10y), AUS200, JP225 1-minute 2005-2020.

The round's one apparent survivor was retracted the same day. Turtle Soup long on the Nikkei (76% win, PF 4.01, t +3.38, max-stat p 0.027 on 2016–2026, n=33) failed its out-of-sample extension: the frozen rule LOSES on independent 2005–2016 data (PF 0.58, t −0.80). Era-specific, not structural — three hours from discovery to retraction. Goal score: 0.5 of 5 (the HSI pre-open fade). Everything else died or measured real-but-untradeable.

02What this round actually taught

  • The drift null is mandatory for long-only daily systems. Battery B's t +3.54 would headline most retail backtests; against randomly-timed long bursts it is unremarkable. Any future long-only daily candidate faces this null first.
  • Refereed anomalies replicate; tradeable ones are rarer. Two academically confirmed effects (Nikkei open conditioning, Gotobi) reproduced on independent data at the predicted sign — and both sit inside the spread. The literature is honest about existence and silent about nets.
  • The meta-law sharpened: slow price discovery makes gold breakouts work, and makes index away-sessions chop. Session speed is not one dial; what the session is slow FOR matters.
  • Stop-run fades keep scoring on Asian indices (HSI pre-open fade, now Nikkei Turtle Soup) and keep failing on US ones — consistent with retail-heavy order books leaving stops where they are visible.
