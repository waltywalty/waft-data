The chart was half telling the truth

Two patterns spotted by eye on the live chart, tested against all five years: H1 (ping-pong) — on non-trending days, a tap of one Asia-range line tends to be followed by a tap of the other; H2 (magnet) — when a range line coincides with a prior day’s or prior NY session’s high/low, price mean-reverts around it. Definitions were pre-registered before the run; 13 economic cells in the ledger, halves split at 2024-01-01, $0.30 costs plus stop slippage.

Verdict: the ping-pong pattern is real as a statistic and worthless as a trade; the magnet premise is measurably false. The other line does get tapped 64% of the time — more on calm days (67%) and stand-aside days (68%), exactly as the eye claimed — but a random walk confined to a trading day already does this, and fading the first tap loses money in every configuration worth taking seriously. Confluent lines get re-crossed exactly as often as non-confluent ones (5.43 vs 5.43 crossings per line): the magnetism is in the eye, not the market.

Out of 1254 days with a first tap, the opposite line was hit before the 16:00-NY close 64% of the time. The splits lean the way the eye said: calmer and stand-aside days ping-pong more. But the gradient over trend deciles is jagged (68 · 66 · 62 · 73 · 66 · 56 · 65 · 58 · 62 · 66 % from calmest to most trending) — a drift of a few points, not a regime. The honest base rate matters here: the Asia range is roughly a third of an average day’s travel, so a day’s ordinary wandering re-crosses it most of the time with no mean-reverting force required.

Why does a 64–68% tap rate not pay? Because the tap statistic says nothing about the path: price often runs well beyond the tapped line first (the breakout our deployed rule trades!), and the 32–36% of days that never come back are exactly the big directional days, so the losses are fat and the wins are capped at one range width. The best cell — calm ∧ stand-aside, PF 1.102, t +0.54, both halves barely positive — is the best of an 8-cell search and sits below both existing watch-list items on a fraction of their evidence. Recorded, not watched.

A range line within 0.10% of yesterday’s high/low or the prior NY session’s high/low gets re-crossed 5.43 times on average before the NY close. A line with no confluence: 5.43. Identical to the second decimal. The hovering you see at confluent lines is selection — the eye checks for confluence precisely on the days price is hovering there.

The revert-to-line trade loses on confluent lines (PF 0.75) and loses harder on the control (PF 0.64) — fading distance from the Asia lines is simply the mirror of the strategy we deploy, and the mirror of an edge is a cost. On stand-aside days it is worse (PF 0.60), not better.

04What survives

The observation skill was genuine — both splits the eye proposed (calm days, stand-aside days) moved the tap statistic the predicted way. What the eye cannot see is the base rate and the path-dependence, which is where the money died. The deployed rule is untouched; the drought remains a filter, not a failure. If a pattern like this is worth another look it will be as a descriptive anomaly first (a statistic that beats its base rate), never as a straight fade.

Method: taps and fades on 5m bars, lines from the 09:30–10:30 HKT range, window to 16:00 NY; trend proxy = |prior 3-day return| vs median (causal); confluence = line within 0.10% of prior HKT-day or prior NY-session high/low; ambiguous target-and-stop bars scored as stops. 13 economic cells.
