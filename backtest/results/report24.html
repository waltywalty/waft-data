research round 24 · the thirteen-name commission · August 2026

Thirteen legends, one testable rule, zero changes

The commission: research thirteen named traders deeply, identify how each actually obtained an edge, and extract whatever can be copied or transplanted. Four research dives (macro legends, systematic camp, pattern camp, and the commentator/vendor remainder) produced verified records, mechanisms, and myth-vs-record separations for all thirteen. Exactly one new well-specified, free-data rule emerged. It was pre-registered, run, and buried. The risk frameworks were transplanted onto our deployed trade set and lost every cell to the sizing we already run.

The deployed rule survives its most famous challengers unchanged. Williams' COT Index — the best-specified free-data idea on the entire list — fails both halves on 2012–2026 weekly gold (max-stat p = 0.76 over 30 counted cells). The Turtle risk layer scores below our stop-distance sizing on every overlay, with intraday pyramiding showing a cleanly negative gradient. The Marcus gap audit measured our worst-case stop overrun at 1.25× intended — benign. One process gate (Unger's average-trade cost floor) is adopted. Goal score stands at 1.5.

02Spec B — the Williams COT Index, replicated and buried

His best-specified idea: stochastic-normalize the commercials' net futures position over 26 weeks; ≥80 is a bullish extreme (trade with the hedgers), ≤20 bearish. We ran his published form exactly — legacy futures-only gold COT, Friday-release alignment, Monday-open execution, 715 tradable weeks 2012–2026 — plus his own WillCo refinement, a large-speculator fade, and a 24-cell gradient, all pre-registered, all counted.

  • The 24-cell gradient is ragged and sign-flipping (+1.57 to −1.61), with the structural 156-week lookback negative everywhere. No smooth slope, so no real effect by the house rule.
  • Max-stat over all 30 counted cells: observed max |t| 1.65, p = 0.76. Costs are not the story (t +0.64 gross). The registered structural-short caveat (miners hedge permanently) was real but not binding — the signal simply carries nothing here.
  • Provenance note: the third-party daily gold series failed its pre-registered cross-check (synthetic weekend rows, ±40–100bp day noise) and was rejected; the test ran on a splice of our own cross-verified feeds (agreement ~1bp, return correlation 0.9995+).

Pyramiding (add every ½N, stops to 2N below the last add) is the clean kill: per-unit expectancy degrades monotonely with allowed units — t +2.50 / −2.22 / −4.51 / −5.23 for max-units 1/2/3/4. A smooth gradient pointing down: adds buy worse prices inside an intraday drift of +$2.28/oz with ~$12 add-spacing. The one interesting nuance: the 2N stop RAISES per-ounce expectancy (PF 1.412, t +3.14, 2% stopped) — replicating our known no-stop result — but risk-sizing off a $49 stop shrinks positions so much the account ends behind. The stop earns its keep through sizing, not through the trades it saves.

04The Marcus audit and the Unger gates

Gap-through-stop tail (descriptive): across 339 stop exits, the realized loss was a median 1.08× the intended stop distance, p99 1.21×, worst 1.25× — so at 1% intended risk the worst single trade cost 1.25% of equity. Largest data gap inside any sampled trade window: five minutes. The limit-lock nightmare that cost Marcus $600k does not apply to intraday spot gold at our size; the existing $0.30 slippage model already covers the observed median.

Adopted from Unger (the one camp whose extract was process, and whose validation checklist maps almost one-to-one onto our house rules):

  • Average-trade cost floor: any candidate must show average net trade ≥ 2× round-trip cost before any other statistic is computed. The deployed rule passes: $1.60/oz vs the $1.20 bar.
  • Market-character pre-test: measure follow-through before choosing breakout vs fade for any new market/session — the round-16/18 cross-market failures were this lesson, learned the expensive way.
  • Incubation: already satisfied — the SPRT boundaries and the journal's live-vs-backtest comparison are exactly his envelope discipline.

05What thirteen names converge on

  • Nobody's verified edge was prediction. PTJ expects to be wrong 80% of the time; Druckenmiller's lesson is sizing; Simons wins 50.75% of trades. Every verified record is a payoff-structure record — which is what our deployed rule already is (40% win rate, 1.96 payoff).
  • The copyable era is gone; the copyable discipline is not. Pit order flow, 1970s supertrends, pegged-currency breaks, Medallion's data moat — none transfer. Stops, sizing, cost floors, and regime gates all do, and we already run stricter versions than most of the list published.
  • Contest returns are max-statistics, not expectancies. Williams, Unger, and Minervini prove skill exists — and their own real-capital records (client fund −$6.1M; “I would never run that risk”; unpublished audits) prove contest numbers are the wrong thing to copy.
  • The sales funnel predicts the evidence quality. Ranked by incentive cleanliness — Bulkowski (books) to Rosputnia (unregulated managed accounts) — verifiability falls almost monotonically. Weight claims accordingly.
