02Fading the New York open

We built the mirror engine — identical trigger, position inverted, stops beyond the break, reversion targets — and proved it exact: on 1,174 matched trades the fade’s P&L is the follow’s negated to machine precision, so any difference in results is the strategy, not the code.

03What practitioners actually do

Three research sweeps, 56 sources: academic papers, independent replications, quant-practitioner backtests, and the retail material itself. The short versions, with what we chose to test:

Opening Range Breakout (ORB)

Define a range from the first N minutes of a session (5/15/30/60 min are the common choices), then trade in the direction the market resolves that range, with a stop tied to the range or to ATR and a time-based exit at end of day. Two distinct lineages exist: Toby Crabel's 1990 futures work (buy/sell stops a "Stretch" away from the open, conditioned on range-contraction patterns like NR7/inside days) and the 2023-24 Zarattini-Aziz papers (a directional first-5-minute-bar rule on QQQ and on high-relative-volume "Stocks in Play", tight ATR stops, 1%-risk sizing under a 4x leverage cap, exit at end of day). As actually traded by practitioners it is a momentum-continuation bet that the open's imbalance persists, and nearly all of the documented edge comes from a minority of large trend days — win rates are ~25-55% depending on variant, with EoD holds or ~10R targets doing the earning.

What has evidence: the Zarattini–Aziz papers (QQQ and “Stocks in Play”) are the only ORB results with audited-quality replication, and the replication is sobering: the QQQ edge is $0.07/share gross and dies at ~2.2¢ of slippage; 76% of the filtered variant’s profit came from 2022 alone. The one filter that beat a placebo was cross-asset confirmation (t=2.05). QuantifiedStrategies found the same ORB rules negative on gold futures — independently corroborating our 117-cell NY null. We tested: their ATR-fraction stops, Crabel’s NR7/inside-day conditioning, relative-volume gating, trigger-time, prior-day-range veto, and the first-bar-direction entry — all transplanted onto the Asia ORB (section 06).

2.6 standard deviation pullback and mean reversion

A counter-trend fade of a price excursion roughly 2.6 standard deviations from a reference mean, entering as price pulls back toward the mean and targeting the mean itself. There is no single canonical "2.6 SD" strategy in the searchable record; the number maps onto three real families: (1) Mostafa Belkhayate's Centre of Gravity system — a gold-famous mean-reversion method whose bands sit at golden-ratio multiples of SD (1.618, 2.618, 4.236) around a repainting polynomial regression, so the "2.6" band is the classic entry zone; (2) generic 99%-confidence bands, since z = 2.576 ≈ 2.6 (Bollinger/linear-regression channels set to ~2.5-2.6 SD, and VWAP band ladders whose 2.51 multiplier is the 98.8% quantile); (3) ICT-style "standard deviation" projections of a session range (multiples of the range, not statistical SDs), where the documented reversal zone is 2-2.5 with 4 as extension. In all variants the trade is: extreme deviation → confirmation of rejection → revert-to-mean target, with an explicit trend/regime filter because band tags in trends are continuation, not reversal.

What has evidence: there is no canonical “2.6 SD” strategy; the number maps onto Belkhayate’s gold-famous Centre-of-Gravity bands (2.618), 99%-confidence Bollinger variants (z = 2.576), and VWAP band ladders. The honest literature is negative for gold specifically: an OU mean-reversion falsification study found every micro-gold-future configuration unprofitable after realistic friction, and Bollinger’s own canon warns that closes outside the band are continuation, not reversion, signals. We tested: the full k-gradient (1.5–3.5σ) across three timeframes and both trigger styles, plus every gate practitioners claim rescues it (section 04).

Judas swing / session-open liquidity sweep with higher-timeframe bias (ICT-style smart money concepts)

The Judas swing is ICT's name for the false move early in a session: a push shortly after a session open that runs resting stops beyond an obvious level (Asian range high/low, London high/low, previous day high/low, equal highs/lows) before the day's real move develops the other way. Canonically it forms between the New York midnight open (00:00 NY) and ~05:00 NY, with the highest-probability window at the London open 02:00-05:00 ET (the 02:00-03:00 "witching hour"); a second variant plays at the NY open, 8:30-10:00 ET, sweeping London/overnight extremes. As traded, it is a stop-run fade: hold a daily-timeframe bias, wait for a counter-bias sweep of an unswept liquidity level, require a lower-timeframe market structure shift (body close, with displacement, ideally leaving an FVG), enter on the retrace, stop beyond the sweep wick, target the opposite side of the swept range and then the previous day's high/low. It is a repackaging of Connors/Raschke's 1995 "Turtle Soup" failed-breakout setup with a session-timing overlay.

What has evidence: nothing audited. ICT’s Judas swing has no verified track record; claimed 55-65% win rates come from discretionary backtests that exclude invalid setups after the fact. The one independent mechanical test we found (OffBeatForex) won 29.6% of take-everything setups and only profited by discarding 92% of them. The canonical spec is real enough to freeze, though: killzone windows (London 02:00-05:00 ET, NY 08:30-10:00 ET), PDH/PDL 4-scenario bias, wick-through-close-back sweeps, stops beyond the sweep wick. We tested: the frozen spec across 45 cells, then the canonical refinements — killzones, PDH/PDL bias, minimum sweep depth (section 05).

06Adapting the research to the strategy that works

08Methods, self-tests, and defects

  • Symmetry proof. The fade engine is verified against the round-7 follow engine: identical trade set, P&L mirrored to 0.00e+00 on 1,174 trades.
  • Base-set drift caught by assertion. The adaptation script initially rebuilt the Asia base with the research-side UTC correlation filter (n=657, PF 1.371); a hard assert against the deployed numbers stopped it, and the script now loads the exact deployed trade set (n=652, PF 1.320) from trades_deployable.pkl.
  • One crash, no silent error. The first Block-B run died on a pandas index-alignment bug in the reporting helper (boolean mask with a fresh index); it produced a traceback, not wrong numbers, and was fixed and re-run.
  • Causality. Sweep levels carry creation and first-crossing times, so “unswept at t” is a lookup, not a scan; daily bias is lag-1 on FX sessions labelled by end date; relative volume compares a completed range window to prior days; every path walk excludes the right endpoint of open-stamped bars.
  • Multiplicity. ≈ 330 tests were run this round and all are reported. No significance is claimed anywhere, so no max-statistic correction is invoked; the two positive leads are named as candidates for forward testing precisely because in-sample subgroup evidence at this test count cannot establish them.
  • The 1-minute caveat stands. Data is 5-minute; the user’s 1-minute confirmation variant remains bracketed, not measured.
