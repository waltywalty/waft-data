The Symphony Portfolio

All the validated and watch-list streams run as one book: the deployed gold rule (1% risk per trade), the Double Seven S&P sleeve, and the HSI pre-open fade (1% risk, capped at a 25% weight because it is an n=43 watch item). The headline finding is the correlation matrix: the three sleeves are statistically uncorrelated (gold–D7 0.00, gold–MHI −0.01, D7–MHI 0.02) — they earn in different markets, different sessions, different mechanisms. That is what makes a portfolio more than its parts.

How to read it. The in-sample optimal split is gold 20% / D7 55% / MHI 25% — the optimizer loads the low-volatility sleeves, which is why the unlevered mix “only” earns 9%: it also only draws down 5.8%. Because Sharpe survives leverage, the same mix at 2× earns 18.4% at an 11.4% drawdown — nearly the gold strategy’s return at two-thirds of its drawdown, and a higher Sharpe (1.38) than anything else on the page. The always-invested variant answers the near-100%-exposure question: 100% S&P buy-and-hold as the base with the strategy mix running on margin on top — 22.8%/yr vs the index’s 13.0% at the SAME ~22% drawdown. Every variant beats holding the index on risk-adjusted terms; which one fits is a temperament choice, not a math one.

03The honest caveats

  • The weights are in-sample. The 20/55/25 split was chosen by looking at this exact window; treat it as a shape (“diversify, cap the watch item”), not a precision dial. With near-zero correlations, any reasonable split captures most of the benefit.
  • The window is friendly to every sleeve. 2020–2025 is gold’s strong era, a mostly-rising S&P (D7's PF 2.28 half), and MHI's only era. The dossier's 20-year D7 curve and the gold rule's era split (+12.8%/yr planning number) are the sobriety checks.
  • MHI is a watch item (43 trades, frozen params, promotion bar at 80) — its 25% cap is a discipline, and its live weight today should be paper-only regardless.
  • The gold sleeve is modeled single-leg. The deployed dual-denominator split (half XAUUSD, half XAUAUD) further stabilizes it (era- invariant Sharpe ~2.2 in round 18) — the real sleeve is slightly better than shown.
  • Leverage and the always-invested base assume margin instruments (CFD/futures) where the strategy sleeves consume little cash; CFD swap on D7's multi-day holds is not modeled.

04The weight-space map (round 28e): there is no sharper optimum

The full simplex at 1% steps — all 5,151 possible splits — under several objectives, plus stability tests. The finding is a plateau, not a peak: 893 splits (17% of all of them) sit within 5% of the best Sharpe, spanning gold 10–33%, D7 26–74%, MHI 0–59%. Naive equal weight (33/33/33) scores Sharpe 1.28 vs the peak's 1.40 — 91% of the optimum with zero optimization.

Why not to chase the decimal: re-optimizing each calendar year gives wildly different answers — 15/75/10 (2021), 75/0/25 (2022), 10/65/25 (2023), 45/55/0 (2024), 45/30/25 (2025). Any single year's “optimum” would have been wrong the next year. A 400-resample block bootstrap puts the optimal weights anywhere in gold 15–40 / D7 40–70 / MHI 0–25. The honest conclusion: any roughly balanced split inside the plateau is statistically the same portfolio — the return comes from the diversification, not the dial. If a single split must be named, the max-MAR corner (roughly gold 35 / D7 40 / MHI 25) is the sensible one: at 2× it maps to ~21%/yr at a ~10% drawdown, marginally better than the max-Sharpe mix on every line.
