XAUUSD · Backtest · 2020-08-21 — 2025-08-01

Asia Open Breakout on Gold

The 09:30 HKT opening-range breakout calls the day's direction correctly 60% of the time. It still doesn't make money — and the reason those two facts coexist is the whole story.

350,903 5-minute bars 1,277 trading days 12 configurations specified $0.30 round-trip cost assumed
Verdict

As specified, the strategy has no tradeable edge. Eleven of your twelve configurations lose money net of costs. The best — a 30-minute range held to the London close — returns a profit factor of 1.026 on 1,276 trades, which is statistically indistinguishable from a coin flip. The worst, a 5-minute range exited at the London open, runs a profit factor of 0.814.

The breakout genuinely does identify the day's direction — but the identification is retrospective. By the time a candle has closed beyond the range, price has already travelled about the width of the range itself, and that distance is almost exactly the size of the edge. You are paying full price for information you are receiving late.

01What was tested

Exactly what you described, with no discretionary latitude added.

  • Range — the first 5 / 15 / 30 minutes from 09:30 Hong Kong time (01:30 UTC; Hong Kong observes no daylight saving, so this is a fixed UTC anchor).
  • Entry — the first same-timeframe candle to close beyond the range high (long) or low (short). One trade per day, first break only.
  • Exit — a fixed clock time in the London session: 07:00 (before), 08:00 (start), 12:00 (middle) or 16:30 (end), London local, so British Summer Time is handled per-date.
  • Costs — $0.30 per round trip, roughly a two-to-three cent spread plus slippage on spot gold. Results at $0.00 through $1.00 are reported below.
  • No stop or target in the base test, since you specified a time-based hold.

On the data. Five-minute XAUUSD bars, 2020-08-21 to 2025-08-01. The timestamps were confirmed to be UTC empirically rather than assumed: the intraday volatility peak shifts by exactly one hour when US daylight saving starts and ends, while staying fixed in UTC. Prices were cross-checked against an independent broker feed (median difference $0.17 across 35,480 overlapping bars) and against the public record — the dataset's all-time high of $3,499.91 on 22 April 2025 matches gold's actual record.

Two patterns are worth more than the individual cells. First, the exit time matters far more than the range length: every configuration that exits at or before the London open loses, and every configuration improves monotonically as the exit is pushed later. Exiting at 07:00 or 08:00 London means closing the position at the exact moment the session that actually moves gold is beginning. Second, longer ranges beat shorter ones — a 5-minute range at the Asia open is mostly noise.

03Does the breakout capture the day's true bias?

Yes — and this is the most interesting result in the study, because it is simultaneously true and useless.

Measured against the 09:30 open, a 30-minute breakout points the right way on 60.2% of days, and a 60-minute breakout on 63.3% — well above the 51.9% you would get by simply being long every day. That looks like a real directional signal, and in a descriptive sense it is.

Measured from the price you are actually filled at, the same signal points the right way on 51.3% of days. A coin flip. The entire apparent edge is the move that has already happened before the confirming candle closes.

The arithmetic is unforgiving. Had you known the eventual breakout direction and been filled at the 09:30 open instead, the same trades would have earned $4.35 per trade at a profit factor of 2.00. Filled at the breakout close, they earn $0.65. You give up $3.70 per trade waiting for confirmation, against an average range width of $4.06. Confirmation costs you the entire range.

The whipsaw figure compounds the problem: after a 30-minute breakout, 71% of days later trade back through the opposite side of the range (it is 87% for the 5-minute range). The Asia range is not a launch pad. It is a chop zone that price re-enters most days.

04Do any confluence filters help?

Twenty-one filters, tested on three range lengths, split into halves. The answer is no, and the way it fails is instructive.

Each filter was evaluated separately in-sample (Aug 2020 – Dec 2023) and out-of-sample (Jan 2024 – Aug 2025). A filter only counts if it beat the unfiltered baseline in both halves. Tested: Asia range size versus ATR, alignment with the daily EMA trend, 5-day momentum, prior-day direction, the overnight gap, breaks of the prior day's high or low, volatility regime, the open's position inside the prior day's range, direction, and day of week.

Fewer filters survived both halves than pure chance would have produced. And the ones that did survive on one range length did not agree with the ones that survived on another. This is the signature of noise, not of a weak signal.

The individual reversals are stark. Aligning with the overnight gap direction produced a 0.82 profit factor in the first half and 1.95 in the second. Trading with the daily EMA trend gave 0.80 then 1.46, while trading against it gave 1.08 then 1.13. Thursday was the worst weekday in-sample and the best out-of-sample. Any of these would look like a discovery if you had only looked at one half.

Entry mechanics did not rescue it either

Since confirmation costs a full range width, the obvious fix is a cheaper fill. Three were tested: a stop order resting at the range boundary with no close confirmation; a limit order back at the boundary after confirmation; and deeper pullback limits. All performed the same or worse. Waiting for a pullback selects against exactly the trending days that pay — at a 1.0-range pullback the fill rate drops to 70% and the profit factor falls to 0.87. The days that come back to your limit are the days that were going to fail.

05What actually moved the needle

Three things changed results materially. Only one of them is an edge, and even that one does not survive scrutiny.

Holding longer

The single largest effect in the whole study. Pushing the exit from the London open to the London close turns a 0.81 profit factor into 0.99 on the 5-minute range, and 0.86 into 1.16 on a 60-minute range. If you trade this at all, exiting before London is the one clearly wrong choice.

Targets, by contrast, consistently hurt: a 1× target lifts the win rate to 81% and the profit factor down to 0.91, which is the classic shape of cutting winners short.

06Why you should distrust the version that looks good

The best configuration found anywhere in this study was a 60-minute range held to the London close: +51.5% cumulative, profit factor 1.16. Here is why I do not believe it.

Three of six years are negative. The result is concentrated in 2021, 2024 and 2025 — and 2024–25 was a violent gold bull market in which simply being long from the Asia open to the London close returned +5.8% and +10.5% respectively without any signal at all.

  • In-sample (2020–23): profit factor 1.06, t = 0.56. Essentially nothing. Out-of-sample (2024–25): 1.44, t = 2.64. The edge does not exist in the first two-thirds of the data.
  • Against passive exposure it adds +43.7%, but with t = 1.18, p = 0.24. It cannot be distinguished from just being long gold during those hours.
  • A randomisation test — keeping every entry and exit time but choosing the side at random, 5,000 times — puts the result at p = 0.004 on its own. Corrected for having searched 18 configurations, p = 0.036. And 18 understates the real search: counting entry modes, filters and risk overlays, this study ran well over a hundred tests. A random search of that size finds a "+48%" configuration 5% of the time with no signal present at all.
  • The session-start sweep shows 09:30 HKT sitting at the peak of the curve (1.16) with 08:00 at 0.95. A structural effect should not be that sharply peaked at exactly the time tested.

None of these is fatal alone. Together they describe a result that is what an unbiased search over five years of gold data produces when there is nothing there.

07Where I would look next

The decomposition points somewhere specific, and it is not at more filters.

  • Fade the range, don't follow it. With 70–87% of days trading back through the opposite side, the structure of this session is mean-reverting, not expansive. The naive inverse also loses to costs — but a fade with a defined stop, entered at the range extremes rather than after a confirmed break, is the hypothesis this data actually supports.
  • Use the breakout as a filter, not a trigger. The direction is right 63% of the time on a 60-minute range. That is a usable bias for something with a better entry — for example a limit order at the London open in the breakout direction, which lets the Asia move happen without paying for it.
  • Trade the London session directly. Every result improves the more of the London session it contains, which suggests the Asia range is a distraction and the London open range is the real object of interest.
  • Condition on the macro calendar. Nothing here separated days with a scheduled US release from days without one, and the volatility profile shows the 08:30 ET release window is where gold's daily variance actually lives.

08Caveats

  • The data ends 2025-08-01. The last twelve months are not covered.
  • The feed is spot XAUUSD from a retail broker. Your fills, spread and swap will differ; a $0.30 round trip is optimistic for retail CFD execution during Asia hours, and at $1.00 even the best configuration falls to a 1.02 profit factor.
  • Results assume a constant one-ounce position. Returns are expressed as a percentage of the gold price so that the 2020 and 2025 price levels are comparable.
  • No overnight financing is modelled. Holds run up to fifteen hours and never cross the 5pm New York rollover, so this is a small omission.
  • Backtests overstate. Assume the live version is worse than what is shown here.
